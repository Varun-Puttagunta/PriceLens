The problem

Overpricing is the most expensive decision in a listing.

An asking price set 5% high does not sell for 5% more. It sits. It collects price cuts, and every cut is a public signal that the seller will move. By the time it closes, it has usually given back more than the optimistic number was ever worth — plus two extra months of carrying cost.

PriceLens models that trade-off explicitly. For every candidate asking price it projects days on market, the price cuts that follow, the carrying cost of the wait and the price the home eventually fetches — and reports the one number that actually matters to a seller: net proceeds.

    How it works

    Five steps, and it shows its work at every one.

    A seller who cannot see why a number is what it is will not trust it. Every stage below is visible in the tool, down to the individual dollar adjustments.

    Fit the market

    A hedonic regression across every closed sale recovers what this market pays for a square foot, a garage bay, a decade of age and a condition grade — and how fast prices are moving, with the mix of what sold held still.

    Find real comparables

    Each candidate sale is scored on location, size, recency, room count, vintage and property type. The search starts tight — a mile and a half, six months — and widens only when that grid comes up short.

    Adjust, line by line

    Every comp is time-adjusted to today, then walked toward the subject one feature at a time. The result is the appraiser's grid: what this comp would have sold for if it were your house.

    Reconcile with a confidence grade

    Comps are weighted by how comparable they actually were, and penalised for needing heavy adjustment. Where they disagree, the range widens and the grade drops — rather than the estimate pretending to a precision it doesn't have.

    Turn value into an asking price

    Value is not a list price. The strategist sweeps candidate prices, projects time on market and net proceeds for each, and picks the number that nets the most — then nudges it under the nearest portal search band so buyers can actually find it.

    What makes it different

    Rates are measured, not assumed

    Most comparative market analyses carry rules of thumb from somewhere else — "a bathroom is worth ten thousand dollars." PriceLens re-fits every adjustment rate from the sales in front of it, so the grid reflects this market this quarter.

    It optimises net, not headline

    The highest asking price and the highest net proceeds are almost never the same number. The strategist prices commission, carrying cost and the discount a stale listing concedes, then recommends against its own vanity metric when the maths says to.

    It publishes its error rate

    Every one of the 36 closed listings is re-priced using only sales that had recorded before it went on the market. That backtest runs live on the results page — including the cases the model got wrong.

    Beyond the tool

    Finding the next place this works.

    Building the pricing tool was the first half of the engagement. The second was advising on where else AI actually earns its keep — and, just as often, where it doesn't. Pricing turned out to be a good first candidate for a specific reason: the decision is repeated, the outcome is measurable within ninety days, and there is a ground truth to check the model against.

    That test — repeated decision, measurable outcome, available ground truth — is now the filter for identifying other companies and workflows worth piloting.

    What makes a good first AI pilot

    1. The decision repeats. One-off judgement calls have nowhere to put a model.
    2. The outcome lands soon. If you learn whether you were right in three months, you can improve. In three years, you cannot.
    3. Ground truth exists. A sale price is a fact. Without one, you are shipping confidence, not accuracy.
    4. Being wrong is survivable. A mispriced listing costs weeks. Start where the downside is recoverable.
    5. Someone owns the decision today. A tool that has no human counterpart has no adoption path.
